# IEEE 802.11 PHY Layer Standards and Their Characteristics

## 1. Introduction

The **IEEE 802.11 standards** define the Physical (PHY) and MAC layers for Wi-Fi networks.  
The PHY layer specifies:
- Frequency band  
- Modulation techniques  
- Data rates  
- Channel bandwidth  

Over time, multiple PHY standards have been introduced to improve speed, efficiency, and reliability.

---

## 2. Major 802.11 PHY Standards

### 2.1 802.11 (Legacy)
- Year: 1997  
- Frequency: 2.4 GHz  
- Data Rate: Up to 2 Mbps  
- Modulation: FHSS / DSSS  

---

### 2.2 802.11a
- Year: 1999  
- Frequency: 5 GHz  
- Data Rate: Up to 54 Mbps  
- Modulation: OFDM  

---

### 2.3 802.11b
- Year: 1999  
- Frequency: 2.4 GHz  
- Data Rate: Up to 11 Mbps  
- Modulation: DSSS (CCK)  

---

### 2.4 802.11g
- Year: 2003  
- Frequency: 2.4 GHz  
- Data Rate: Up to 54 Mbps  
- Modulation: OFDM  

---

### 2.5 802.11n (Wi-Fi 4)
- Year: 2009  
- Frequency: 2.4 GHz & 5 GHz  
- Data Rate: Up to 600 Mbps  
- Features:
  - MIMO (Multiple Input Multiple Output)  
  - Channel bonding (40 MHz)  

---

### 2.6 802.11ac (Wi-Fi 5)
- Year: 2013  
- Frequency: 5 GHz  
- Data Rate: Up to ~6.9 Gbps  
- Features:
  - Wider channels (80/160 MHz)  
  - MU-MIMO  
  - Higher modulation (256-QAM)  

---

### 2.7 802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6 / 6E)
- Year: 2019  
- Frequency:
  - 2.4 GHz  
  - 5 GHz  
  - 6 GHz (Wi-Fi 6E)  
- Data Rate: Up to ~9.6 Gbps  
- Features:
  - OFDMA  
  - Target Wake Time (TWT)  
  - 1024-QAM  
  - Improved efficiency in dense environments  

---

### 2.8 802.11be (Wi-Fi 7)
- Year: ~2024 (latest)  
- Frequency:
  - 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, 6 GHz  
- Data Rate: Up to ~46 Gbps  
- Features:
  - 320 MHz channels  
  - Multi-Link Operation (MLO)  
  - 4096-QAM  
  - Ultra-low latency  

---

---

## 4. Key Evolution Trends

### 4.1 Increasing Data Rates
- From **2 Mbps → 46 Gbps**
- Achieved using:
  - Higher modulation schemes (QAM)
  - Wider channels
  - Multiple spatial streams

---

### 4.2 Better Spectrum Utilization
- Transition from DSSS → OFDM → OFDMA
- More efficient handling of multiple users

---

### 4.3 Advanced Technologies
- **MIMO / MU-MIMO** → Multiple devices simultaneously  
- **Channel bonding** → Wider bandwidth  
- **OFDMA** → Efficient multi-user access  

---

### 4.4 Improved Performance in Dense Environments
- Especially in:
  - Universities  
  - Airports  
  - Stadiums  

---

## 5. Summary

- Early standards (a/b/g) focused on **basic connectivity**
- 802.11n introduced **MIMO and higher speeds**
- 802.11ac improved **throughput and bandwidth**
- 802.11ax and 802.11be focus on:
  - **Efficiency**
  - **Low latency**
  - **High-density performance**

---

## 6. Conclusion

The evolution of 802.11 PHY standards reflects the growing demand for:
- Higher speeds  
- Better reliability  
- Efficient spectrum usage  

Modern standards like **802.11ax and 802.11be** are designed to handle **high user density and next-generation applications**, making them ideal for environments like universities and smart campuses.
